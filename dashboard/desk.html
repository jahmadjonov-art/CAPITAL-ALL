The model

Probability that BTC settles above the strike, under a driftless lognormal walk: P = Φ(d₂) with d₂ = [ln(S/K) − σ²τ/2] / (σ√τ). Volatility is measured from recent one-minute returns, not assumed.

Why the gap is probably the model, not the market

  • Settlement is not spot. These contracts resolve on a CF Benchmarks index averaged over the final minute. Averaging cuts terminal variance, so the true fair value is pushed toward the extremes relative to this model. This alone can explain several cents and is the first thing to fix.
  • Recent volatility is not future volatility. σ comes from the last few hours of one-minute bars. Over a 15-minute horizon that estimate is noisy, and the model is most sensitive to it near the money.
  • Zero drift is assumed. Defensible over minutes, but it is an assumption, not a measurement.
  • A large persistent gap on a liquid contract is a red flag. The market has more information than this model does. When they disagree by more than a cent or two, the prior should be that the model is wrong.

What would have to be true before any of this is tradable

  • Venue. Everything here is Kalshi-direct. The funded account is Robinhood, whose event pricing is roughly 4¢ per round trip against a 1¢ spread. Until that is resolved, a gap this desk finds is not reachable.
  • Edge must survive costs and queue. A maker fill earns the spread; a forced taker exit costs 1.75¢ per contract, about 1.75 round trips of profit.
  • The model must be calibrated before it is trusted. Record predicted probability against realised outcome over many contracts. If the 30% bucket does not settle yes about 30% of the time, the gap column is noise.
