Unusual option volume · day / week / month

What this measures, and what it does not

Unusual here means volume against open interest. A contract trading many times the open interest that stood there at last night's close is new positioning rather than existing positions changing hands. That is the standard screen and it is the honest version of the question we can answer with free data today.

The share-volume badge is a different, fully measured thing. Where a ticker shows shares 96th pct that is "unusual for this name" — its share volume for this session ranked against its own previous six months, off two years of history. A name can be busy in options while its shares are quiet, or the reverse, and the two together say more than either alone.

The same measure for OPTION volume is being banked, not backfilled. It is the one number this sheet most wants and the one that cannot be bought: there is no endpoint that gives a ticker's total option volume for a past day, and rebuilding it contract by contract would take thousands of requests per name. Doing it per contract instead does not work either — sampling this sheet's own standouts, the median contract had five days of history, because near-dated contracts have not existed long enough to have any. So each run records the day, and the count below shows how far that record has got. Nothing can recover the days before it started.

Open interest is yesterday's number. It is published once, after the close, and does not move during the session — which is exactly what makes the ratio mean something, but it also means a scan run before the open is describing the previous session.

Excluded: contracts that are not really options
